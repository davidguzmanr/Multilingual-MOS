What annotators see

One task rendered from labeling_config.xml and tasks.json (clip 1). Hints appear in HumanSignal when hovering an option; here they are printed under each option.

You will hear a short speech clip in English, which may be a real recording or computer-generated, together with the text it is supposed to say. Listen to the whole clip, then answer two separate questions. Naturalness is about how the voice sounds: does it sound like a real person speaking? Judge only the sound, not whether the words are correct. Intelligibility is about the words: are all the words clear and the same as in the text? Listen while reading the text and count the words that are unclear, wrong, missing, added or repeated, or cut off. Judge the spoken words, not the spelling: different spelling or missing accent marks in the text are not errors, and a regional accent is not an error if it is still the same word. If a wrong tone or a wrong vowel length makes it a different word, count it as a wrong word. A word you cannot make out counts as an error even if you can guess it from the text. Answer each question on its own: a natural voice can say the wrong words, and a poor voice can say the right words.

Printing, then, for our purpose, may be considered as the art of making books by means of movable types.
